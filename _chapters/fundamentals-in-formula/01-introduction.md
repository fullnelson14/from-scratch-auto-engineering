### Who this book is for

This first book is about the bare minimum knowledge about FSAE for the absolute beginner. For those that are interested in learning FSAE but have no idea where to start. For those that want to design a race car and build a race car and race a race car, for those that are intrigued by the challenge and excited by the endeavor. This book assumes that the only thing that you have seen before now is pictures of open-wheeled race cars. And if you haven’t seen those before:

### Book Overview

At the end of this book, you should understand 

1. What the Formula Student program and competition is (Part 1)
2. What the cars are like and what systems are on them (Part 2)
3. Your role as a participant, the crucial early milestones for a new team, and what you can expect while participating in your first year (Part 3)

This book is about establishing the right expectations for all of our discussions in this series. It doubles as a ‘first introduction’ to Formula. By reading through this book, you’ll be well prepared for the more specific lessons and details in other books. These basics are the base assumptions that I use - a way for us to agree on the terms and themes of the sport. 

When this book is released and for a time after, I'll only be focusing on the electric class of Formula Student. That's where I spent my time, so that is what I know. In due time, I’ll add the relevant details for an engine based powertrain (which is, in truth, my preferred powertrain). Most of the principles in this series apply to all Formula vehicles and teams. 

I'm writing this book because I lost track of how many times I wished I had a book like this: a book that was straightforward and easy for a beginner to understand. A book that I could hand my team leads to be able to all start with the same expectations and assumptions about the formula program and the directions that we were heading. A book that helped unify us in our efforts and helped us all start with the same baseline. A book that aggregated the right resources for the right people.

Being the founder of the formula team at my school, I needed a way to understand as much about the car as I could. While the many rabbit holes that I ended up going into were valuable, I wish there was a book that aggregated more useful information with much fewer dead ends. That is what this book is trying to be: the minimum set of information that you need to understand what Formula Student is. 

### Disclaimer on Naming

I refer to the series as Formula Student in my writing. In the beginning, and to this day in the US, the competition is called Formula SAE, which refers to the governing body that organizes the entire program (Society of Automotive Engineers). I find that Formula Student is easier to read, a little quicker to type, and conveys the vision of what the program is aimed at well. I mean no disrespect to the OGs - FSAE will always be the real title. Plus, after a few chapters, I just use “Formula” to refer to it anyway. 

To start, let’s dive into the main characters of the series: the cars.  

## Introducing: The Template Car

In this book and in this series, we'll focus on what I am calling the template car. It’s purpose is to be the minimum viable vehicle that is the cheapest possible, can enter the competition, safely pass technical inspections, and race all of the competition events. Said another way, the template car is the bare minimum that you will need to do in order to participate in everything that the FSAE program has to offer. 

Because the template car is meant to be the simplest version of what an FSAE car could be, that means a lot of the bells and whistles and the knowledge required to design them will not be covered here. I include it right away in this first book because it establishes an important baseline understanding of Formula Student vehicles. Once you have that baseline, many other parts of the series will make much more sense.

The template car is comprised of:

- Frame: welded steel tubes (called a “space frame”)
- Powertrain: a single motor driving a limited-slip differential through a chain
- Suspension: direct acting (no pushrods)
- Aerodynamics: none

Some will have a problem with approaching FSAE in this way, but I think you'll find that most teams that have tried to start a program in recent years and most participants that are multi-year veterans of the sport will have the same advice for newcomers and new teams. That advice is to stick to the simplest possible plan that will still allow you to participate. Making the simplest car possible will allow you more time to work on other things, which you will need in the beginning. Starting out, you’ll need to learn a lot of surrounding details, which will take up time during the year. Making your car the simplest that it can be gives you the highest probabilities of participating in the highest amount. The template car allows you to start with a crawl, then a walk. Then, when you have learned how, you’ll be able to run. 

### Purpose of Formula Student

Formula is a wonderful program. It's a program about growth and will take you further in your skills than you could ever go on your own. It's a team-based program where you're striving after a long-term, extremely hard endeavor.  In the age of AI degrees and where knowledge is becoming increasingly more commonplace, I believe that Formula and the programs like it are one of the remaining unique things that a university can offer: a team-based, long-term, objective-driven endeavor focused on the growth of the individual. This is where I believe Formula really shines as a program: as a vehicle (pun intended), for growth for each individual participating on the team.

I also believe that Formula is accomplishing its true purpose when it is touching the most amount of lives that it possibly can. You'll see in later books that means I will strongly advocate for recruiting to as wide of an audience as you possibly can and removing as many barriers to entry as possible. Believe me when I tell you that the knowledge barriers to entry are high enough already. The rules state that the purpose of the Formula Student Program is to allow student teams to act like an engineering firm that designs and builds a formula-style race car. You'll notice that it doesn't exclusively target engineers. There is and will always be a place for more business-minded individuals. Even though much of the focus of the Formula Program is on the engineering activities of designing a car, it is not all of the Formula Program. I believe that the highest leverage activities for a program's success long-term are to be found within the business functions of your team, not the engineering functions.
