### Purpose & Vision

The purpose of FSAE is to turn you into a better engineer through the hardest challenge possible: designing a racecar. If you think about it, Racing is one of the closest sports on earth to ‘competitive engineering’, where your creation is pitted directly against other teams. If there are inefficiencies in ANY part of your design, the racetrack will find them, and tax you for them in the form of higher lap times. This is a great way to build engineers who can deeply understand how to build a great product, because you are directly rewarded for finding better ways to do things.

SAE groups Formula into a category called “Collegiate Design Series”, which is their label for all of the competitions that they organize (including SAE Baja, SAE Snowmobile challenge, SAE Aero Design, etc). For Formula, the first rule in the rulebook lays out their purpose well. Take a look from the 2026 rulebook:

![Formula SAE rulebook — purpose of the competition]({{ "/assets/images/fundamentals-in-formula/fsae-rulebook-purpose.png" | relative_url }})

So regardless of whatever else you achieve as a participant in the Formula Student series, you should understand that first and foremost it is a program that is aimed at helping you grow into a stellar engineer. The last sentence in this rule also hints at the evidence that it works: companies recruit very enthusiastically from Formula Student teams, and you gain instant connections to industry engineers that are alumni of the series. In other words, the program is effective at doing what it sets out to do (helping you grow into a skilled engineer, which are highly desirable to companies), and they have the evidence to back it up (there are many companies that recruit heavily from Formula Student).

### Challenge

The best way to grow is to take on the hardest challenge available to you. Formula tries its best to represent that by tasking student teams with designing and building a racecar within one year. The competition is designed in a way that aims to award the most points to the team that does the best job of designing and building a racecar. In this way, the Formula Student program provides the tools to students for their growth. Follow the logic here: by setting up the point system and competition to reward the best racecar, teams will prioritize designing the best racecar they possibly can. This means that regardless of where you are in your engineering skills, if you strive to deepen your knowledge and apply the engineering principles to your racecar, you will have a better racecar, thereby doing better at the competition. It is a way to hold up a meter stick that everyone can measure their progress by. 

A favorite analogy of mine is that Formula Student is like a really good gym. The purpose of a gym is to spend time and energy to become more fit than you were. Input energy, output muscle. My favorite part is: you don’t need to hold the record for heaviest weight lifted in order to grow. I’d argue that the best goal for a Gym is to stretch each participant a little further than they could go before they started coming, regardless of where the starting and ending results line up to compared to everyone else. That is exactly what Formula sets out to do. Of course, you should strive to be the top performing team (that’s the entire challenge of racing - who can be the best?), but Formula Student does a fantastic job at empowering growth in each participant, regardless of where they started and where they ended. 

Formula is a gladiator’s arena that will transform you into the best engineer that you can be. It will be brutally demanding, but the payoffs are immensely rewarding. To drive home the point: it doesn’t require top finishes in order to empower your growth. How can I know that? Simple. I grew more as an engineer participating in Formula Student than I ever had before, and my team finished in 58th place. I promise you that with committment and buy-in, your experience will be the same. No top finish required.  

FSAE is a challenge where teams of students from universities are tasked with designing and building a single seater, open-wheel, formula-style racecar. There are some rules (mostly about keeping the person driving the vehicle safe) that must be adhered to if your car is to be allowed out on track during the annual competition, which is a gathering of all the teams and cars. Your car is put through rigorous safety checks and inspections, an equally rigorous design critique from industry professionals, and several physical tests to evaluate its performance as a racecar. The crowning event is the Endurance Race, where you and many other cars will be out on track working to complete as many laps as you can within 30 minutes or so. 

For all of these events, there are points awarded, and the team with the most points at the end of the weekend is crowned the winner.
